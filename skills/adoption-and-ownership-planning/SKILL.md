# Adoption And Ownership Planning

## When to Use
- A recommendation requires people to change behavior, approve decisions, operate controls, or sustain a new way of working.
- The issue involves unclear ownership, trust gaps, resistance, stakeholder sensitivity, or governance.
- The output affects controls, governance, policy, standards, audit evidence, financial reporting, or risk acceptance.

## Critical Governance Rule
When a recommendation affects controls, governance decisions, policy, standards, compliance posture, ownership, approvals, audit evidence, financial reporting, regulated data handling, or risk acceptance, treat adoption and ownership as critical. Do not leave ownership, decision rights, evidence, or monitoring informal.

Policies, procedures, controls, standards, processes, and governance artifacts may evolve, but ownership includes responsibility for routing changes through the appropriate lifecycle, approval, evidence, and review path. Use `finance-documentation-lifecycle` when governed document changes require controlled revision, publication, versioning, retention, or retirement.

## Human-in-the-Loop Control Gate
If a recommendation may create, revise, retire, reinterpret, or materially affect a control or governance artifact, do not proceed on assumptions. Request explicit user instruction or affirmation before drafting or changing policy, procedure, process, control, ownership, approval, evidence, or governance language. If intent is ambiguous, ask for clarification.

## Workflow
1. Identify the sponsor, accountable owner, contributors, reviewers, approvers, and affected stakeholders.
2. Clarify decision rights and escalation path.
3. Name the behavior change required for adoption.
4. Identify likely resistance, trust gaps, incentives, and communication needs.
5. Define trust-building moves and feedback channels.
6. Specify operating responsibilities after the initial fix or pilot.

## Quality Checklist
- Owner and decision rights are explicit.
- Adoption is tied to behavior change, not just communication.
- Resistance and trust are treated as system signals, not personal defects.
- Governance or control impacts include evidence, approval, and monitoring ownership.
